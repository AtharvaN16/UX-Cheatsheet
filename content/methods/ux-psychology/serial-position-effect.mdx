## What is it

People recall the first (Primacy) and last (Recency) items in a series best, while middle items are easily forgotten.

## Why it matters

In navigation design, landing pages, and multi-step workflows, information order dictates user recall and action rates. Placing critical navigation items or primary value propositions in the middle of a long list guarantees low engagement, whereas placing them at the beginning or end ensures maximum retention.

## Key ideas

- **Primacy Effect**: Items at the beginning of a list are rehearsed and transferred into long-term memory.
- **Recency Effect**: Items at the end of a list are still fresh in active short-term working memory.
- **The Middle Trough**: Information presented in the middle of a sequence suffers from high cognitive interference and lowest recall.

## How it works

The Serial Position Recall Curve:

```
Memory Recall (%)
 100% ┼─── [Primacy]                                         [Recency] ───┼
      │                                                        /         │
  50% │                                                       /          │
      │         ─────────────── [Middle Trough] ─────────────/           │
   0% └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴
      First Item                     Middle Items               Last Item
```

## Example

1. **Mobile Bottom Navigation Bars**: Apps like Instagram place *Home* at the far-left (first position - Primacy) and *Profile* at the far-right (last position - Recency), placing less critical tabs like Search and Reels in the middle.
2. **E-Commerce Product Features**: Highlighting top differentiators in bullet points #1 and #2, followed by secondary specs in the middle, and ending with warranty/guarantee details in the final bullet.

## Application

- **Position Key Navigation Items**: Place critical destinations (Home, Search, Account) at the extreme ends of navigation menus and tab bars.
- **Optimize Onboarding Carousels**: Show the main value proposition on slide 1 and the primary sign-up CTA on the final slide.
- **Structure Sales Copy**: Put compelling benefits at the top of landing pages and repeat strong CTA summaries at the very bottom.

## Limitations

- **Visual Contrast Override**: A middle list item that features strong visual isolation (Von Restorff Effect) will break the serial position trough and achieve high recall.
- **Domain Familiarity**: Power users who know a list by heart bypass position bias and scan directly for specific targets.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Middle items are never read"**: Middle items are scanned and processed; they simply suffer from lower spontaneous retrospective memory recall.
- **"Lists should only have 3 items"**: Longer lists are fine as long as critical items occupy the primary anchor slots.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Reordering navigation menus and bullet points based on click-stream conversion analytics.
- Summarizing long document content into structured key takeaways framed with strong primacy and recency anchors.

**Where it fails**
- Predicting user intent when items have equal situational importance.
