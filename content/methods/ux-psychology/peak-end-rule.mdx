## What is it

People judge an experience based almost entirely on how they felt at its peak and at its end, rather than the total sum or average.

## Why it matters

Users do not average their experiences mathematically. A journey with 9 neutral steps and 1 delightful final touchpoint creates a vastly higher retrospective rating than a journey that was mildly good throughout but ended on a frustrating bug.

## Key ideas

- **Peak Intensity**: The highest positive or negative emotional moment dominates memory encoding.
- **Final Impression (The End)**: The closing moments of a session or workflow heavily influence whether a user returns or recommends the product.
- **Duration Neglect**: The actual length of an experience has minimal impact on how it is recalled.

## How it works

Retrospective Evaluation Model:

```
[Start] ────► [Step 1] ────► [PEAK MOMENT (High Impact)] ────► [Step 3] ────► [THE END (High Impact)]
                                       │                                              │
                                       └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                                              ▼
                                               Retrospective Memory Evaluation
```

## Example

Rideshare applications like Uber turn a potentially stressful payment step into a seamless background event at the end of the trip, leaving the passenger with a delightful final impression as they exit the vehicle.

## Application

- **Celebrate Accomplishments**: Design delight into success screens (e.g., confetti animations, positive confirmation copy upon completing a major project).
- **Graceful Error Recovery**: Turn negative peaks (system errors or failed payments) into helpful, empathetic recovery flows.
- **Smooth Offboarding**: Ensure sign-out, task completion, and subscription management end on a clear, reassuring note.

## Limitations

- **Does Not Substitute for Core Functionality**: A great final animation cannot rescue a product that is fundamentally broken or useless.
- **Negative Peaks Distort Memory**: A single severe security or data-loss peak can permanently ruin brand perception regardless of how delightful the end step is.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Every single screen must be delightful"**: Trying to make every screen a peak causes design fatigue. Reserve peak moments for key milestones.
- **"Length of workflow matters most"**: Reducing a flow from 4 steps to 3 matters less than ensuring the final step feels fast and rewarding.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Analyzing user sentiment across journey touchpoints to identify emotional peaks and drop-off points.
- Generating empathetic error recovery copy for high-friction moments.

**Where it fails**
- Predicting exact human emotional peaks without real qualitative observations.
