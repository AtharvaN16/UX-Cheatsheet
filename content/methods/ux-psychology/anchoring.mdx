## What is it

People rely disproportionately on the first piece of information encountered (the "anchor") when making subsequent decisions and price evaluations.

## Why it matters

In digital product pricing, landing pages, and value propositions, the first number a user sees sets their baseline perception of expense. Displaying a $200/month enterprise plan first makes a $49/month pro plan feel like an inexpensive bargain.

## Key ideas

- **First Impression Benchmark**: The initial value fixes a reference point in working memory.
- **Relative Valuation**: Humans judge prices and metrics relatively against the anchor rather than objectively in isolation.
- **Crossed-out Price Anchors**: Displaying original strike-through prices ($99 $49) anchors valuation to the higher original figure.

## How it works

Perceived Value Transformation:

$$\text{Perceived Bargain Magnitude} = \text{Initial Anchor Value} - \text{Current Offered Price}$$

```
[ High Initial Anchor ($200/mo) ] ──► [ Current Offered Price ($49/mo) ] ──► Perceived High Value & Low Risk
```

## Example

1. **Strike-Through E-Commerce Pricing**: Displaying ~~$120~~ **$79** on product pages anchors the user's value perception to $120, making $79 feel like a clear saving.
2. **Enterprise Pricing Tier Sequence**: Placing the high-tier *"Enterprise Plan ($299/mo)"* on the far-left of pricing tables anchors customer expectations, increasing conversion on the middle *"Pro Plan ($79/mo)"*.

## Application

- **Display High-Value Tiers First**: List premium packages first in pricing tables to set a high anchor value.
- **Show Struck-Through Original Prices**: Display pre-discount prices clearly during sales promotions.
- **Provide Suggested Donation / Tip Anchors**: Pre-select tier buttons ($25, $50, $100) instead of presenting a blank custom input field.

## Limitations

- **Absurd Unrealistic Anchors**: Setting an outrageously inflated anchor (e.g., ~~$10,000~~ **$19**) triggers user skepticism and loss of brand credibility.
- **Repeated Purchase Context**: Frequent buyers who know true market prices bypass anchoring bias.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Anchors must be prices"**: Star ratings, initial review counts, download numbers, and default numbers all serve as anchors.
- **"Anchoring deceives users"**: Highlighting true original value helps users evaluate real cost savings.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Evaluating pricing page layouts to ensure optimal visual placement of value anchors.
- Generating A/B test variations for donation and tier anchor values based on user segment data.

**Where it fails**
- Determining moral and legal thresholds for fair pricing anchors without human review.
