## What is it

The tendency to search for, interpret, and recall information that confirms prior beliefs while ignoring contradictory evidence.

## Why it matters

Designers and UX researchers frequently fall prey to confirmation bias when testing their own feature designs. Researchers ask leading questions (e.g., *"Was this new navigation easier to use?"*) or interpret ambiguous participant hesitations as validation of their own design ideas, leading to shipping flawed products.

## Key ideas

- **Selective Observation**: Noticing user actions that agree with your design hypothesis while dismissing mistakes as "user error."
- **Leading Question Formatting**: Structuring research questions to steer participants toward desired responses.
- **Hypothesis Attachment**: Falling in love with your design solution makes critical feedback feel like a personal attack.

## How it works

Filtered Research Cycle:

```
[ Prior Design Hypothesis ] ──► [ Filtered Observation (Ignore Flaws) ] ──► False Research Validation
```

## Example

1. **Leading Usability Testing Questions**: Asking a participant *"Did you like how simple that checkout was?"* instead of open-ended neutral prompting like *"How was your experience completing that purchase?"*
2. **Feature Analytics Cherry-Picking**: Focusing on a 5% increase in button clicks while ignoring a 15% drop in total checkout completion on the same page.

## Application

- **Write Open-Ended Test Protocols**: Use neutral phrasing (*"Walk me through what you are thinking as you look at this screen"*).
- **Formulate Falsifiable Hypotheses**: State upfront what quantitative metric result would *disprove* your design hypothesis.
- **Conduct Blind Peer Synthesis**: Have an independent researcher who did not build the prototype code and analyze user interview recordings.

## Limitations

- **Subconscious Vulnerability**: Knowing confirmation bias exists does not make researchers immune to it—formal structural protocols are mandatory.
- **Over-Correction Risk**: Dismissing valid positive user feedback out of fear of confirmation bias can derail successful product launches.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Senior researchers don't suffer from confirmation bias"**: Seniority increases confidence, which can actually strengthen confirmation bias unless peer protocols are enforced.
- **"Data analytics eliminates confirmation bias"**: Analysts can easily select time windows or metrics that confirm their pre-existing opinions.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Auditing user interview scripts for leading phrasing and biased question structures.
- Synthesizing qualitative transcript sentiment using neutral objective classification models.

**Where it fails**
- Replacing human researcher self-awareness and ethical research rigor.
