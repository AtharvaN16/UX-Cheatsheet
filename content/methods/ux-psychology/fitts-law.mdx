## What is it

The time required to rapidly move to a target area is a function of the ratio between the distance to the target and the width of the target.

## Why it matters

Target size and distance dictate physical user effort, pointer accuracy, touch precision, and error rates across desktop and mobile devices.

## Key ideas

- **Target Size Matters**: Larger click/touch targets are faster and easier to acquire.
- **Distance Matters**: Targets placed closer to the user's current cursor/finger location take less time to hit.
- **Infinite Outer Boundaries**: Screen edges and corners have infinite effective target size because the cursor cannot overshoot them.

## How it works

Mathematical formula:

$$MT = a + b \cdot \log_2 \left( \frac{2D}{W} \right)$$

Where:
- $MT$ is Movement Time.
- $D$ is Distance to the target center.
- $W$ is Width of the target along the axis of motion.
- $a$ and $b$ are empirical constants.
- $\log_2(2D/W)$ is the Index of Difficulty ($ID$).

## Example

Mobile navigation bars fixed to the bottom of the smartphone screen leverage Fitts's Law because they reside directly under the user's thumb, minimizing distance $D$ and maximizing interactive surface area $W$.

## Application

- **Expand Touch Targets**: Ensure minimum interactive touch areas of 44×44 pt (iOS) / 48×48 dp (Android).
- **Position Actions Near Context**: Place contextual action menus (like hover actions or context menus) directly next to the triggering item.
- **Leverage Edges**: Place primary controls at screen boundaries or corners on desktop interfaces.

## Limitations

- **Doesn't Model Multi-Touch Gestures**: Fitts's Law models linear pointing movements; it does not accurately model complex gestures like pinch-to-zoom or multi-finger swipes.
- **Overly Large Targets Diminish Hierarchy**: Making everything huge to maximize target size degrades visual hierarchy and layout efficiency.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Only the visual button size counts"**: The hit area (clickable padding) is what determines $W$, not just the visible icon or label text.
- **"Fitts's Law applies identically to touch and mouse"**: Finger touch targets require larger margins due to thumb occlusion and lower pointing precision compared to sub-pixel mouse cursors.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Auditing UI design files to detect undersized touch targets (e.g., buttons under 44px).
- Simulating mouse movement distance maps across high-frequency task flows.

**Where it fails**
- Accounting for physical ergonomic comfort (e.g., one-handed thumb reachability zones on varying mobile screen sizes).
