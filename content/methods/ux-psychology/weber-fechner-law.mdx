## What is it

The Just Noticeable Difference (JND) in sensory stimulus is directly proportional to the initial magnitude of the stimulus.

## Why it matters

A $5 price increase on a $10 item feels huge (50% increase), triggering intense user outrage. However, a $5 increase on a $1,000 laptop is completely unnoticeable (0.5% increase). 

Similarly in UI design, increasing font size from 12px to 14px (+16%) creates a striking visual difference, whereas increasing font size from 48px to 50px (+4%) goes completely unnoticed. Visual scales and price adjustments must follow proportional ratios rather than fixed mathematical steps.

## Key ideas

- **Just Noticeable Difference (JND)**: The minimal change in stimulus magnitude required for human senses to perceive a difference.
- **Logarithmic Human Perception**: Perception scales exponentially; equal physical increments produce diminishing perceived differences at higher baseline values.
- **Proportional Redesign Thresholds**: Incremental UI changes below the JND pass unnoticed; major redesigns exceeding JND require careful change management.

## How it works

Mathematical Formulation:

$$\Delta I = k \cdot I_0 \quad \implies \quad S = k \cdot \ln(I)$$

Where:
- $\Delta I$ is the Just Noticeable Difference (JND)
- $I_0$ is the initial stimulus intensity
- $k$ is the Weber constant for a specific sensory domain
- $S$ is perceived sensation

## Example

1. **Typography Modular Scales**: UI design systems use proportional scales (e.g., Major Third 1.25x or Perfect Fourth 1.33x: 12px, 16px, 21px, 28px, 37px, 50px) to ensure every font step exceeds the perceptual JND threshold.
2. **SaaS Price Tiering**: Raising a $15/mo plan to $18/mo (+20%) requires announcing new feature value to offset perceived JND shock, whereas raising an enterprise contract from $50,000 to $51,000 (+2%) passes without friction.

## Application

- **Use Proportional Typography Scales**: Define font sizes using geometric ratios rather than fixed +2px increments.
- **Package Price Increases Proportionally**: Keep subscription price updates under the 5–8% JND threshold to minimize customer churn.
- **Design High-Contrast Visual States**: Ensure hover, active, and focus states exceed the visual JND color contrast threshold.

## Limitations

- **Complex Cognitive Choices**: The law applies directly to sensory perception (brightness, sound, weight, price); complex multidimensional choices involve higher cognitive heuristics.
- **Individual Sensory Threshold Variance**: JND thresholds vary slightly between users with visual impairments or color blindness.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Fixed linear increments produce equal visual steps"**: Adding 2px to a 10px font is noticeable; adding 2px to a 60px headline is invisible.
- **"Users notice every minor design tweak"**: Subtle UI tweaks under the JND threshold pass completely unnoticed by end users.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Calculating proportional typography, spacing, and elevation scale tokens for design systems automatically.
- Auditing UI screens for color contrast shifts that fall below visual JND accessibility thresholds.

**Where it fails**
- Predicting human emotional outrage triggered by perceived unfair price changes.
