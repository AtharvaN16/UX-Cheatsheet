## What is it

A small set of named characters, each standing for a pattern of behaviour and goals found across real research participants, adopted as the specific person a team designs for instead of "the user".

## Purpose

Cooper's argument in *The Inmates Are Running the Asylum* was about scope, not empathy. An elastic "user" stretches to accommodate any feature, because someone, somewhere, might want it; a named person with a specific working day does not. The persona's real job is to let a team say no — the strongest case for them, and the first thing most persona projects forget.

## When to use

- Work gets justified with "some users might want this" and nobody challenges it
- You have research, and behaviour diverges across audiences in ways that change the design, not the marketing
- Several teams build one product while privately imagining different audiences

## Structure

Everything in a persona should be able to change a decision.

- **Behaviour** — what they do now, including the workaround and the tool they use instead of yours
- **Goals** — the outcome they want, and the decision they are trying to make
- **Context** — where, when, under what interruption, on whose approval
- **Vocabulary** — their words for the thing, for labels and copy
- **Abandonment** — what makes them revert

The name and photo are memory aids, not evidence; age, income and favourite coffee are decoration that makes an assumption look like a finding. Three to five personas, or nobody holds them.

## How to use

- Cluster coded research on behavioural variables, not demographic ones
- Give each persona a scope — which decisions it informs — and revisit it when the audience moves
- Use them out loud in review: name the persona a feature serves, and the ones it doesn't
- Record which attribute rests on which evidence, so a disputed one gets checked, not argued

## Example

A team building rota software for home-care agencies derives three personas from 14 interviews. The coordinator persona's load-bearing line is not her age but that she rebuilds the week in a spreadsheet each Friday, distrusting the in-app totals — so the dashboard is dropped and export fixed first.

## Common mistakes

- **Demographics standing in for behaviour** — "35, urban, two children" changes nothing you would build; the three open tabs and the 7am shift change everything.
- **Made once, never opened again** — Flaherty's account of persona failure is mostly this: built in a silo, handed over, never invoked in a decision, then cited as evidence research is waste.
- **One persona per stakeholder** — twelve personas is the elastic user again, with photographs.
- **Marketing segments relabelled** — purchase-intent segments answer who to target, not what to build.

## Limitations

- **No demonstrable validity.** Chapman and Milham's central argument: a persona can be neither verified nor falsified. It is admittedly fictional, so no data disproves it, and inconvenient evidence gets dismissed as irrelevant.
- **Coverage is unknown.** Each added attribute narrows the group described, by an amount nobody can calculate without correlation data — their illustration, on deliberately simple assumptions, puts a 21-attribute persona at about 130 people in the US. Nor can qualitative personas say what share of your users each represents, so "our primary persona" is a claim about attention as much as evidence.
- **Politically usable.** Since the underlying data is rarely inspectable, a persona wins arguments rather than settling them — how a synthesis artefact ends up blocking the research that would correct it.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Clustering coded transcripts into candidate behavioural patterns, and showing which attributes co-occur
- Interrogating a draft: per attribute, ask what decision changes if it were different, then cut the ones with no answer

**Where it fails**
- Generating personas from a product description. The output is fluent, complete and formatted exactly like a researched persona — the danger, given personas are already hard to falsify. It strips out the evidence that made a good one trustworthy, and looks identical.
