## What is it

A written claim explaining *why* a pattern in your research exists — precise enough to act on, and precise enough to disagree with. One or two sentences, carrying its evidence and a confidence level.

## Purpose

A study decays into anecdote unless somebody writes down what it meant. Insight statements are the unit that survives the deck, and writing one forces a vague team feeling into a claim explicit enough to be wrong.

## When to use

- You have clustered qualitative data and need to turn themes into claims a team can act on
- A decision is imminent and stakeholders need the *why* behind a behaviour, not a tour of nine sessions
- You are stocking a research repository and need atomic claims that outlive the project they came from

## How to do it

Keep three things apart; conflating them is the whole problem.

- **Observation** — what you saw, session by session: "six of nine participants re-read the fee line before confirming." No interpretation.
- **Finding** — the pattern across observations: "people hesitate at the fee total." True, and not yet useful.
- **Insight** — why the pattern exists, put so a different action follows: the total lands after people anchored on the item price, so the fee reads as a penalty rather than a cost, and re-reading is people checking they are not being tricked.

Then, per insight:

- Name the mechanism or tension, not the effect. If you cannot say *because*, you have a finding.
- Attach the evidence — ids, quotes, timestamps — so anyone can audit the claim back to data. An untraceable insight is an opinion with a citation style.
- Mark a confidence level and what it rests on: participant count, segment spread, agreement with other data.
- Apply the disagreement test: if a colleague who knows the domain cannot construct a plausible counter-claim, you wrote a description.

## Example

A subscription team studying churn expects a pricing problem. Observation: most interviewees cancelled days after an unfamiliar renewal email. Finding: notices trigger cancellation. Insight: the email lands after weeks of non-use, so it works as a usage audit rather than a bill — pointing at re-engagement, not discounting. Confidence tentative, one segment.

## Common mistakes

- **Relabelling a finding as an insight** — "users find checkout confusing" names no mechanism and implies no action, so everyone leaves with a different idea of what to do.
- **Writing only comfortable insights** — if every statement confirms what the team believed, synthesis has become ratification. The valuable ones cost somebody an assumption.
- **Dropping the evidence trail** — a claim circulated without its sessions cannot be re-checked, and gets quoted long after it stopped being true.
- **Promoting one vivid participant** — a compelling story is a hypothesis; it becomes an insight when it recurs, or when you can say why it generalises.

## Limitations

- An insight is an inference, not a measurement. The mechanism you name is the best available explanation of the data, and stays a hypothesis until something tests it.
- Explanatory power and evidence strength vary independently: the most interesting statement in a set often rests on the fewest participants, so confidence has to be written down rather than implied by tone.
- Quality is bounded by the questions asked; synthesis cannot recover a mechanism nobody asked about.

## Tips

Write the statement with *because* in it, then delete the word. If the sentence stops making sense, you had a finding.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Drafting competing explanations for an established theme, to check against transcripts rather than adopt
- Auditing a finished set for the restatement failure: which statements name no mechanism and imply no action

**Where it fails**
- Generating insights straight from transcripts. A model produces fluent statements that read exactly like insight, *because* clause included, since fluency is what it optimises. What it cannot supply is the part that matters: an explanation that survived the contradictory detail in the data. Ask for the observations behind anything it hands you.
