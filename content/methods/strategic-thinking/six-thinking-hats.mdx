## What is it

A structured group technique where the whole group deliberately adopts one "thinking mode" at a time — represented by a colored hat — instead of everyone arguing from whatever mode they default to. The point is forcing the group through the same mode together, one at a time.

## Purpose

Group discussions naturally converge on one dominant mode — usually critical evaluation, sometimes unchecked optimism — because that's whoever's loudest or most senior defaults to. De Bono's method, which he called "parallel thinking," forces everyone into the same mode simultaneously and then rotates, so the group gets the benefit of every mode (facts, emotion, caution, optimism, creativity, process) in sequence rather than one mode dominating by social dynamics.

## When to use

- A group discussion keeps degenerating into premature critique before ideas get a fair hearing, or into unchecked enthusiasm with no real scrutiny.
- You want to deliberately separate emotional reactions, factual analysis, and creative exploration instead of letting them blur together in one unstructured debate.
- Design reviews or brainstorms where the same few people always play the same role (the critic, the optimist) — the hats force everyone to occupy the other roles for once.

## How to do it

1. Introduce the six hats and what each represents: **White** (facts and available information only), **Red** (feelings, intuitions, gut reactions — stated without justification), **Black** (caution, risks, what could go wrong), **Yellow** (optimism, benefits, best case), **Green** (creativity, new ideas, alternatives), **Blue** (process — managing the discussion itself, usually the facilitator's hat).
2. Have the entire group wear the same hat at the same time, one hat per discussion segment — not different people wearing different hats simultaneously, which recreates the usual dynamic where roles get fixed to specific people.
3. Move through the hats in an order suited to the discussion — a common sequence is White (facts) → Green (ideas) → Yellow (benefits) → Black (risks) → Red (gut check) → Blue (decide next steps) — but the order can be adapted to the situation.
4. Keep each segment focused strictly on its mode; someone raising a risk during the Yellow-hat segment gets redirected, not engaged, until Black hat comes around.
5. Close with Blue hat: summarize what came out of each mode and decide what to do with it.

## Common mistakes

- **Letting people self-select their "natural" hat and stay there** — this recreates exactly the fixed-role dynamic (the permanent critic, the permanent optimist) the method exists to break.
- **Skipping Black hat because it feels negative** — caution and risk are a full sixth of the method for a reason; skipping it produces the same unchecked-optimism failure mode the technique is meant to prevent.
- **Running it without a facilitator enforcing the mode** — without someone holding the group to the current hat, discussions drift back into whatever mode dominates by default within a few minutes.
- **Using it for a decision that needs one person's final call** — the hats structure exploration and idea generation; they don't replace an actual decision-rights mechanism like DACI or RAPID for who commits to the final call.

## Tips

Use Blue hat explicitly at both the start (to set the agenda and sequence) and the end (to convert what surfaced into next steps) — a group that explores all six modes but never returns to Blue hat has generated good material with no path to using it.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Running a solo version of the exercise — prompting a model to respond to the same idea in each hat's mode in turn — as a way to surface angles a single person might not generate alone before bringing the idea to a group.

**Where it fails**
- The method's real value in a group setting comes from making six *different people's* actual, differently-biased perspectives visible in turn — a single model simulating six hats is one perspective performing six voices, not six independent viewpoints, and it can produce fluent-sounding coverage of every mode while missing the genuine disagreement real people would surface within the same hat.
