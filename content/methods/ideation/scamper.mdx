## What is it

A checklist of seven prompts — Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse — applied one at a time to an existing product or idea to force a deliberate variation on it.

## Purpose

Forces variation on an idea that's gone stale by making you answer a specific question instead of freely brainstorming, which tends to loop back to the same handful of obvious ideas. Each prompt targets a different kind of change, so running through all seven systematically covers more of the possibility space than an open-ended "any other ideas?" would.

## When to use

- You have an existing product, feature, or process and need variations on it, not a blank-page idea
- A brainstorm has stalled and the group keeps circling the same two or three ideas
- You want a structured way to make sure a session covers substitution, combination, and removal — not just addition
- You're short on time and want a checklist that produces output fast without much setup

## How to do it

1. Pick the specific thing to vary — a feature, a step in a flow, a whole product — and state it plainly.
2. Go through the seven prompts one at a time, forcing at least one answer to each before moving on: Substitute (what can be swapped out?), Combine (what can be merged with something else?), Adapt (what can be borrowed from elsewhere?), Modify/Magnify/Minify (what can be emphasized, shrunk, or exaggerated?), Put to another use (who else could use this, for what?), Eliminate (what can be removed entirely?), Reverse/Rearrange (what can be flipped or resequenced?).
3. Capture every answer, even the obviously bad ones — the checklist works by volume, and a weak answer to one prompt sometimes sparks a strong one at the next.
4. After a full pass, review the list and mark answers worth prototyping or exploring further.
5. Repeat the pass on a narrower part of the problem if the first round stayed too high-level.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping prompts that "don't seem to apply"** — the prompts that feel awkward for your problem are often the ones that produce the most novel answer, precisely because they're not the direction you'd have gone naturally.
- **Stopping after one or two strong ideas** — the point is to complete the full checklist; stopping early defeats the purpose of forcing coverage across all seven kinds of change.
- **Treating every answer as a finished idea** — SCAMPER produces raw prompts to explore, not vetted concepts; each answer still needs to be evaluated and developed.
- **Running it on a problem with no existing thing to vary** — SCAMPER transforms something that already exists; for a genuinely blank-page problem, it has nothing to operate on.

## Tips

Run it against a specific, narrow "thing" — one feature, not "the whole product" — each pass. A checklist applied too broadly produces vague answers to every prompt; applied narrowly, each prompt produces something concrete enough to actually act on.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Generating a first-pass list of answers to each of the seven prompts for a described product or feature, as raw material for a team to react to and build on
- Running the checklist quickly across many small features when time doesn't allow a live group session

**Where it fails**
- Treating a model's SCAMPER output as the finished idea set. The prompts are meant to spark a human's next thought — a generated list that's never reacted to or built on by the team just recreates generic, averaged answers instead of the specific variation the exercise is meant to surface for your actual product.
