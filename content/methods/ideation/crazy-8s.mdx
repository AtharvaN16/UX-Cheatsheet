## What is it

Each participant folds a sheet of paper into 8 panels and sketches 8 distinct variations of a solution in 8 minutes — one minute a panel — to force past the first, most obvious idea before committing to anything.

## Purpose

The first idea anyone sketches for a problem is reliably the least interesting one — it's whatever's already familiar. Crazy 8s exists to burn through that first idea fast and force 7 more variations out under real time pressure, on the bet that the later panels, made with no time to second-guess, contain the more interesting divergence.

## When to use

- You already have a specific, narrowed problem or "How Might We" statement — Crazy 8s diverges on solutions to a defined problem, it doesn't help define the problem itself
- You want visual, concrete concepts rather than written or verbal ideas
- The group has already been through a broader ideation pass and needs to push from concept to sketchable form
- You're running a design sprint or similarly time-boxed workshop and need a fast individual-then-share exercise

## How to do it

1. Make sure the group is aligned on a single, specific problem statement first — Crazy 8s on an unfocused prompt just produces 8 vague sketches instead of 8 sharp ones.
2. Have each participant fold a sheet of paper into 8 equal panels (fold in half three times).
3. Set a strict 8-minute timer — some facilitators use 8x1-minute intervals with a bell between each, others let people pace themselves across the full 8 minutes.
4. Each person sketches a different variation of a solution in each panel — rough, ugly sketches are the point; reassure non-designers explicitly that these don't need to look good.
5. At time's up, move directly into sharing: each person presents their favorite one or two panels to the group, then the group votes (dot-voting works well here) on which concepts to develop further.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping straight to Crazy 8s without a narrowed problem** — without a specific prompt from a prior diverge/converge pass, participants sketch 8 different problems instead of 8 solutions to the same one.
- **Letting people slow down and polish a panel** — the time pressure is load-bearing; a facilitator who lets the clock slip loses the exact mechanism (forced fast iteration) that makes this work.
- **Judging sketch quality in the share-out** — the method measures idea variety, not drawing skill; critiquing artistic execution shuts down the people who most need the psychological safety of "ugly sketches are fine."
- **Running it solo without a group share-out after** — individual sketching is only half the method; the value compounds when the group sees everyone's range side by side and votes.

## Tips

Do a round of individual Crazy 8s before any group discussion of solutions, not after — hearing other people's ideas first collapses everyone's 8 panels toward whatever was said out loud.

## Using AI

**Where it helps**
- Turning a chosen Crazy 8s panel into a cleaner mockup afterward, once a human has picked the direction — a fast way to communicate a sketch without spending more workshop time on fidelity.

**Where it fails**
- Generating the 8 variations themselves. The forced-iteration pressure and the participant's own hand doing the sketching are the mechanism — a model asked for "8 variations" produces 8 plausible-sounding options with none of the individual designer's specific constraints baked in, and skips the exact struggle (pushing past your own first idea) the exercise is designed to create.
